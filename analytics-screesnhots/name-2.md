# Presentation material — our original two candidates (Sonar, Bluewater)

Written in the same format as `name-1.md` (Kingfisher, Bluefin) so all four can sit
side by side in one deck: **the story → why the name earns it → how it sounds in the
wild → identity → the honest risk.**

---

## SONAR — ❌ ELIMINATED (see honest risk below)

### The story (slide narrative)

> Sonar doesn't search the ocean. It sends out a signal, and waits.
>
> Most of that signal disappears into open water and never comes back — that's the
> vast majority of everything happening out there, and it's not worth chasing. But
> when the signal hits something real, it echoes back, and only then do you know
> exactly where it is.
>
> LinkedIn works the same way. Every post you publish sends out a signal — a like, a
> comment, a moment of real attention — into an ocean of noise: bot comments, empty
> reactions, reach numbers that mean nothing. Most of it never echoes back.
>
> We don't search that ocean by hand. We listen for what echoes back, and tell you
> exactly where the real people are.
>
> We don't generate leads. We read signals. And we'd rather find you ten real echoes
> than a thousand empty ones.

### Why the name earns it

- **It is the most literal mechanical description of the product available.** Sonar
  is quite simply what the product does — send a signal, read what echoes back — which
  makes it very easy to explain in one sentence to a buyer.
- **Carries Story 2 and Story 5 directly**, and Story 4 (one real echo vs. a
  thousand empty ones) with almost no translation needed.
- **Short, one word, easy to say** in most languages — "sonar" is a near-identical
  loanword across English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese.

### How it sounds in the wild

- *Sonar — we don't search LinkedIn. We listen for what echoes back.*
- *Every post sends a signal. Sonar tells you which ones are real.*
- Product vocabulary that comes for free: **a ping** (a tracked post going live),
  **an echo** (a qualified signal), **signal vs. noise** dashboards.

### Identity

Dark, technical, radar-sweep visual language; a single pulse/echo ring as a mark.
Reads more "engineering-grade instrument" than "content" brand — a deliberate choice,
since precision/expertise (Story 5) is the register it leans into hardest.

### The honest risk — disqualifying, not just a footnote

**SonarSource** (makers of SonarQube, a well-known code-quality SaaS company) holds
multiple registered US trademarks on "Sonar" and "Sonar-formative" marks for software
and SaaS (e.g. SONARSOURCE, SONARCLOUD, and the bare mark SONAR, Reg. No. 6,132,431),
and **has actively sued another company** — Sonar Software, Inc. — in federal court
(Delaware, case 1:23-cv-01053) for trademark infringement, purely for using "Sonar" as
a company/product name in the software/SaaS category. SonarSource is demonstrably
litigious about this exact mark in this exact category. Using "Sonar" (or close
variants) for another B2B SaaS product carries real legal exposure, not just a
crowded-search-results problem. **Disqualified — dropped from the candidate list.**

## BLUEWATER

### The story (slide narrative)

> "Blue water" is a sailing term. It means the open ocean — far from shore, out of
> sight of land — as opposed to the safe, shallow coastal water anyone can sail in a
> weekend. Sailors who go there are the ones who know what they're doing.
>
> LinkedIn looks calm on the surface: a feed, a profile, a like button. Underneath,
> it's open water — hundreds of millions of profiles, currents of attention moving in
> every direction, no map. Most tools keep you close to shore, skimming the surface
> for whatever easy, shallow contacts they can find.
>
> Real content deserves real expertise. Bluewater sailors don't hug the coast — they
> read the water, trust their instruments, and go where the easy tools won't. That's
> where the real people are: past the noise, past the shallow engagement, in the deep
> water where genuine interest actually lives.
>
> We help serious teams sail into the depth of LinkedIn, and come back with the people
> who actually matter — not the easy, shallow catch.

### Why the name earns it

- **Carries Story 5 almost perfectly** — the whole "blue water sailing" concept *is*
  the "LinkedIn is an ocean, finding the right people takes real expertise" story,
  practically word for word.
- **Quiet nod to LinkedIn's brand color** ("blue water") without ever naming the
  platform — the same trick as Kingfisher's electric-blue plumage, done through water
  instead of an animal.
- **Reinforces the "not for everyone" positioning** — blue-water sailing is explicitly
  a skill level, not a hobby anyone picks up; maps onto the "high-end, sophisticated
  buyer" segment described in the brief.

### How it sounds in the wild

- *Bluewater — for teams who don't sail the shallow water.*
- *Anyone can skim the surface. Bluewater goes where the real people are.*
- Product vocabulary: **depth** vs. **shallow engagement**, **the crossing** (a
  tracked campaign), **making harbor** (reaching a qualified meeting).

### Identity

Deep ocean blue/navy, horizon-line visual language, a compass-rose or single wave
motif. Reads premium and calm — closer to a private-banking or yacht-brand register
than a typical SaaS dashboard, which fits the "not the best price, the best product"
buyer described in the brief, but is worth stress-testing against whether it reads as
too far from "software" at first glance.

### The honest risk

Real, but softer than Sonar's. "Bluewater" is already in active B2B software use by
at least two unrelated companies: **Bluewater Control** (an enterprise SaaS platform
for mobile/telecom expense management, ISO 27001 accredited, hundreds of clients) and
**Bluewater Software** (an apparel/ERP software company, NYC). Neither is in a
remotely adjacent category and neither appears litigious, but the `.com` and the plain
wordmark are both almost certainly unavailable, and "Bluewater" plus "software"
already returns unrelated B2B SaaS companies on the first page of search results —
worth a real trademark clearance check before shortlisting, and likely requires a
modified domain/wordmark (e.g. a single word variant, a suffix, or a different
spelling) rather than the bare word.
