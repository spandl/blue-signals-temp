# Presentation material — the two finalists

Both sections below are written to be read aloud off a slide. Structure is identical so the
two can be compared side by side in the same deck: **the story → why the name earns it →
how it sounds in the wild → identity → the honest risk.**

---

## KINGFISHER

### The story (slide narrative)

> There is a bird that hunts on water nobody else can read.
>
> The surface of a river is glare, ripples and movement — noise. From above, you cannot see
> what's under it. Most birds do the obvious thing: they wade in the shallows and take
> whatever is easy to reach.
>
> The kingfisher doesn't. It holds still above the noise, corrects for the distortion of the
> water, waits — and then dives once, and comes back with exactly the fish it chose.
>
> LinkedIn is that river. Every post you publish disappears into glare: likes that mean
> nothing, comments written by bots, reach numbers that flatter nobody. Somewhere under that
> surface are the few people who actually read what you wrote, twice, and cared.
>
> Content is still king. Our job is to find who's listening to it — and to come back with
> the ten who matter, not the thousand who don't.
>
> That's Kingfisher.

### Why the name earns it

- **It carries two of our stories at once.** "Content is still king" (Story 1) is *inside the
  name* — Kingfisher literally means the one who fishes for the king. And the bird's whole
  behaviour is Story 4: one precise dive, not a net.
- **It rejects the category.** Every competitor name sounds like volume — lists, pipelines,
  scrapers. A patient, precise hunter says the opposite thing before anyone reads a word of
  copy.
- **It's concrete, not clever.** Nothing to decode. The kingfisher exists in almost every
  market we sell into and is a well-loved bird — Eisvogel, martin-pêcheur, martín pescador,
  kuningaskalastaja, ijsvogel. A non-native speaker doesn't need the metaphor explained;
  they've seen the animal.
- **It looks premium.** The bird is famously the most vivid blue in European wildlife — a
  legitimate reason for the identity to live in LinkedIn's colour space without ever saying
  "LinkedIn."

### How it sounds in the wild

- *Kingfisher — content is still king. We find who's listening.*
- *Kingfisher reads the noise on your content and hands you the ten people who actually cared.*
- *Ten real ones beat a thousand fake ones. Kingfisher finds the ten.*
- Product vocabulary that comes for free and needs no glossary: **the dive** (a run against a
  post), **surfaced** people, **still water** vs. **glare**, **the catch**.

### Identity

Electric-blue-on-dark, one silhouette, held mid-hover. Nature photography rather than
illustration — this is a real animal doing something genuinely hard, and photography sells
"real" the way our whole positioning does. Works down to a favicon: one blue bird shape.

### The honest risk

Kingfisher plc — the UK retail group behind B&Q and Screwfix — owns the word broadly in
retail. Completely unrelated class, no buyer overlap, but it will dominate a naive Google
search for "Kingfisher" and it means the `.com` is almost certainly unavailable. Plan on a
modified domain and a real trademark opinion in class 9/42. Secondary note: it's the longest
name of the finalists (11 letters, three syllables).

## BLUEFIN

### The story (slide narrative)

> Most of what lives in the sea lives close to shore, in warm shallow water where anything
> can reach it. That's where the easy catch is, and that's where everyone fishes.
>
> The bluefin doesn't live there. It lives in the deep, cold open ocean — fast, rare, and
> further out than the easy boats go. It's also the single most valuable fish in the world.
> One bluefin is worth more than an entire hold of what you'd scoop up near the coast.
>
> LinkedIn has the same geography. There's the shallow water — the like counts, the
> impression numbers, the contacts anyone can buy in bulk — and then there's the deep water,
> where the few people who genuinely care about what you published actually are.
>
> Most tools keep you near shore, because that's easy. We built the instruments to go out
> past that, and to come back with the one that's worth the trip.
>
> That's Bluefin.

### Why the name earns it

- **It is the value proposition as an object.** The bluefin isn't just rare, it's *the premium
  one* — the fish where a single specimen beats a whole net. That's Story 4 stated without
  argument, which is exactly what you want in a name.
- **It keeps Bluewater's best idea and fixes its worst problem.** You clearly like the
  deep-water/expertise territory. "Blue water" needs a sailing lesson before it lands;
  "Bluefin" needs nothing — it's a thing, not a term.
- **"Real, not generated."** A bluefin cannot be manufactured or farmed at scale the way a
  contact list can be bought. It maps cleanly onto Story 3: we find people, not leads.
- **The blue is doing quiet work.** "Blue" is right there in the name — a soft LinkedIn signal
  to anyone who notices, invisible to anyone who doesn't, and legally safe.
- **Best pronunciation profile of any finalist.** Two syllables, two words a non-native
  speaker already knows, zero spelling ambiguity when said on a call.

### How it sounds in the wild

- *Bluefin — past the shallow water, where the real people are.*
- *Anyone can fish the shallows. Bluefin goes where your actual audience is.*
- *We don't sell you a net. We find you the one that's worth the trip.*
- Product vocabulary: **deep water** vs. **the shallows**, **the run**, **depth**, **a bluefin**
  as the internal word for a genuinely qualified person.

### Identity

Deep navy, cold and expensive-looking, with one silver-blue form and a lot of empty space —
visually the quietest, most premium option on the table, and the one that most looks like it
belongs in a boardroom deck rather than a growth-hack blog. Very strong single-form logo mark.

### The honest risk

Two known holders in tech-adjacent space: **Bluefin Payment Systems** (payment security, US,
sells to enterprise) and **Bluefin Robotics** (undersea vehicles, defence). Neither competes
with us, but Bluefin Payments is a real B2B software brand and shares a broad buyer
population, which makes the trademark question genuinely open rather than academic. `.com` is
taken. Secondary note: as a food-luxury name it carries an overfishing/sustainability
association that a hostile reader could turn against a "we don't take everything, we take the
right one" brand — worth deciding whether to address it head-on or never mention fishing
pressure at all.

## Side by side, if the room needs a call

| | Kingfisher | Bluefin |
|---|---|---|
| Story fit | Strongest — carries Story 1 *and* Story 4 | Strong — Story 4 + Story 5 |
| Emotional register | Skill, patience, craft | Rarity, value, prestige |
| Explains itself | Instantly (an animal doing something hard) | Instantly (the expensive rare one) |
| Non-native friendliness | Good — 3 syllables | Best — 2 syllables |
| Ownability of the image | Very high — hover, dive, blue flash | High — deep water, single form |
| Legal cleanliness | Big unrelated holder (Kingfisher plc) | Two adjacent-ish tech holders |

**My recommendation:** Kingfisher, if the trademark opinion comes back workable. It's the only
name that puts "content is still king" *inside* the word while also dramatising precision, and
the identity territory is more distinctive than Bluefin's. Bluefin is the safer, more
boardroom-neutral pick and the better one if you want the premium/scarcity note to dominate
over the craft note — and it's the stronger name on pure pronounceability, which matters at
60% non-native.
