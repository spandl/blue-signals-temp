# Name & Stories

## The core idea / positioning

- **Content is still king**, even in an age of AI-generated posts and automated outbound. Genuine, well-crafted content still makes a real person stop, read, and respond.
- **We are not a lead generator.** A "lead" is a name on a purchased list. We find real people — people with a real problem, a real interest, who reacted to specific content because it actually meant something to them.
- **Real people have good spam filters.** The people worth reaching have already learned to ignore ads, automation, and cold outreach. If they still engage, that engagement is meaningful — and hard to fake.
- **Quality over quantity, deliberately.** Ten real, qualified matches are worth more than a thousand cold contacts — the literal meaning behind the internal code name "Top 10%."
- **LinkedIn is an ocean.** Millions of profiles, constant noise, no map. Finding the handful of people who genuinely resonate with a specific piece of content, by hand, is effectively impossible. This product is the instrument that makes it possible.

## The five brand stories

**Story 1 — "Content is still king. Everything else is noise."**
Everyone can publish. Everyone can automate. AI can write a post in seconds, and bots can comment on a thousand more. But none of that creates a real connection. Real content — written by people who know their craft — still has power. It still makes people stop scrolling. It still makes people answer. We believe content is still king. Our job is to find who's listening to it.

**Story 2 — "Every post sends a signal. We find who catches it."**
When you publish something real, it doesn't just sit there — it reaches people. Someone reads it twice. Someone clicks your profile. Someone comments something that shows they actually understood it. That's a signal. Most tools only count it. We read it, and we find the person behind it.

**Story 3 — "We don't find leads. We find people."**
A lead is a name on a list. A person is someone with a real problem, a real question, and a reason to care about what you wrote. Leads can be bought in bulk. People can't. We build the tools to find the real ones — the ones who read your content because it actually spoke to them.

**Story 4 — "Ten real ones beat a thousand fake ones."**
Most tools are built to find as many contacts as possible. We think that's the wrong game. One person who truly cares about your content is worth more than a thousand who don't. We'd rather hand you ten of the right people than a thousand of the wrong ones.

**Story 5 — "LinkedIn is an ocean. Finding the right people takes real expertise."**
LinkedIn looks small on a screen — a feed, a profile, a like button. It isn't. It's an ocean of hundreds of millions of profiles, constant motion, no map, no way to search it by hand. Most tools just skim the surface, grabbing whatever is easiest to reach. Finding the handful of people who actually matter, in all that noise, takes real instruments and real expertise. That's what we built.

## Naming brief given to external research

Guidelines used when sourcing further name candidates:
- Premium/high-end B2B SaaS tone — credible to sophisticated buyers, not gimmicky or playful.
- ~60% of users are non-native English speakers — prefer simple, concrete, easy-to-pronounce names over abstract/academic terms. A little intellectual flavor is fine (content/marketing audience), but nothing that needs explaining.
- Should evoke at least one of: content/quality still mattering despite automation; finding real people vs. generated "leads"; precision/quality over mass volume; the difficulty of finding the right people in a vast, noisy space ("LinkedIn as an ocean").
- Optional: a subtle nod that the product is built for LinkedIn, without naming it directly (color association with LinkedIn blue, wordplay on "-in", etc.).
- Avoid anything that reads as generic "lead generation"/"sales prospecting" (leads, prospect, pipeline, outreach) — contradicts Story 3 directly.
- Basic conflict-check against existing, reasonably well-known products before shortlisting.

## Live candidates

### KINGFISHER

**The story:**
There is a bird that hunts on water nobody else can read. The surface of a river is glare, ripples and movement — noise. From above, you cannot see what's under it. Most birds do the obvious thing: they wade in the shallows and take whatever is easy to reach.

The kingfisher doesn't. It holds still above the noise, corrects for the distortion of the water, waits — and then dives once, and comes back with exactly the fish it chose.

LinkedIn is that river. Every post you publish disappears into glare: likes that mean nothing, comments written by bots, reach numbers that flatter nobody. Somewhere under that surface are the few people who actually read what you wrote, twice, and cared.

Content is still king. Our job is to find who's listening to it — and to come back with the ten who matter, not the thousand who don't. That's Kingfisher.

**Why the name earns it**
- Carries two stories at once: "content is king" is inside the name (Kingfisher = the one who fishes for the king), and the bird's hunting behavior is Story 4 — one precise dive, not a net.
- Rejects the category — competitor names sound like volume (lists, pipelines, scrapers); a patient, precise hunter says the opposite before anyone reads a word of copy.
- Concrete, not clever — the bird exists in nearly every market we sell into (Eisvogel, martin-pêcheur, martín pescador, kuningaskalastaja, ijsvogel) and needs no metaphor explained.
- Looks premium — the most vivid blue in European wildlife, giving a legitimate reason to live in LinkedIn's color space without ever saying "LinkedIn."

**How it sounds in the wild**
- *Kingfisher — content is still king. We find who's listening.*
- *Ten real ones beat a thousand fake ones. Kingfisher finds the ten.*
- Free product vocabulary: **the dive**, **surfaced** people, **still water** vs. **glare**, **the catch**.

**Identity:** Electric-blue-on-dark, one silhouette mid-hover, nature photography over illustration. Works down to a one-shape favicon.

**Honest risk:** Kingfisher plc (UK retail group, B&Q/Screwfix) owns the word broadly in retail — unrelated class, no buyer overlap, but dominates naive search and the `.com` is almost certainly unavailable. Plan on a modified domain + trademark opinion in class 9/42. Longest name of the finalists (11 letters, 3 syllables).

### BLUEFIN

**The story:**
Most of what lives in the sea lives close to shore, in warm shallow water where anything can reach it. That's where the easy catch is, and that's where everyone fishes.

The bluefin doesn't live there. It lives in the deep, cold open ocean — fast, rare, further out than the easy boats go. It's also the single most valuable fish in the world; one bluefin is worth more than an entire hold of what you'd scoop up near the coast.

LinkedIn has the same geography: shallow water (like counts, impression numbers, contacts anyone can buy in bulk) and deep water, where the few people who genuinely care about what you published actually are.

Most tools keep you near shore, because that's easy. We built the instruments to go out past that, and come back with the one that's worth the trip. That's Bluefin.

**Why the name earns it**
- The value proposition as an object — the bluefin isn't just rare, it's the premium one (a single specimen beats a whole net) — Story 4, stated without argument.
- Keeps Bluewater's best idea, fixes its worst problem — "blue water" needs a sailing lesson to land, "bluefin" needs nothing, it's a thing.
- "Real, not generated" — cannot be manufactured/farmed at scale the way a contact list can be bought — maps cleanly onto Story 3.
- "Blue" does quiet work — a soft LinkedIn nod, invisible to anyone who doesn't notice, legally safe.
- Best pronunciation profile of any finalist — two syllables, two known words, no spelling ambiguity on a call.

**How it sounds in the wild**
- *Bluefin — past the shallow water, where the real people are.*
- *We don't sell you a net. We find you the one that's worth the trip.*
- Free product vocabulary: **deep water** vs. **the shallows**, **the run**, **depth**, "a bluefin" as internal shorthand for a genuinely qualified person.

**Identity:** Deep navy, cold and expensive-looking, one silver-blue form, lots of empty space — the quietest, most premium option, reads boardroom rather than growth-hack blog. Strong single-form logo mark.

**Honest risk:** Two tech-adjacent holders — Bluefin Payment Systems (payment security, enterprise B2B) and Bluefin Robotics (undersea vehicles, defence). Neither competes directly, but Bluefin Payments shares a broad buyer population, making the trademark question genuinely open. `.com` is taken. Also carries an overfishing/sustainability association a hostile reader could turn against the brand — worth deciding whether to address head-on or avoid the fishing angle entirely.

### BLUEWATER

**The story:**
"Blue water" is a sailing term: the open ocean, far from shore, out of sight of land — as opposed to the safe, shallow coastal water anyone can sail in a weekend. Sailors who go there know what they're doing.

LinkedIn looks calm on the surface — a feed, a profile, a like button. Underneath, it's open water: hundreds of millions of profiles, currents of attention moving in every direction, no map. Most tools keep you close to shore, skimming the surface for whatever easy, shallow contacts they can find.

Real content deserves real expertise. Bluewater sailors don't hug the coast — they read the water, trust their instruments, and go where the easy tools won't. That's where the real people are: past the noise, past the shallow engagement, in the deep water where genuine interest actually lives.

We help serious teams sail into the depth of LinkedIn, and come back with the people who actually matter — not the easy, shallow catch.

**Why the name earns it**
- Carries Story 5 almost perfectly — "blue water sailing" practically *is* "LinkedIn is an ocean, finding the right people takes real expertise," word for word.
- Quiet nod to LinkedIn's brand color without naming the platform — same trick as Kingfisher's plumage, via water instead of an animal.
- Reinforces "not for everyone" positioning — blue-water sailing is a skill level, not a hobby anyone picks up; maps onto the high-end, sophisticated buyer.

**How it sounds in the wild**
- *Bluewater — for teams who don't sail the shallow water.*
- *Anyone can skim the surface. Bluewater goes where the real people are.*
- Free product vocabulary: **depth** vs. **shallow engagement**, **the crossing** (a tracked campaign), **making harbor** (reaching a qualified meeting).

**Identity:** Deep ocean blue/navy, horizon-line visual language, compass-rose or single wave motif. Premium and calm, closer to private-banking/yacht-brand register than a typical SaaS dashboard — worth stress-testing whether that reads as too far from "software" at first glance.

**Honest risk:** Real but softer than Sonar's. Already in active B2B software use by two unrelated companies — Bluewater Control (enterprise SaaS, mobile/telecom expense management, ISO 27001, hundreds of clients) and Bluewater Software (apparel/ERP, NYC). Neither adjacent or litigious, but `.com` and the plain wordmark are almost certainly unavailable, and "Bluewater" + "software" already surfaces unrelated B2B SaaS companies on page one of search. Needs real trademark clearance and likely a modified domain/wordmark.

## No-go list

- **Resonance** — Resonate.com is an established audience-intelligence company selling to the same marketing buyer; "Resonance/Resonate/Reso-" is crowded generally.
- **Sonar** — SonarSource holds registered US trademarks on "Sonar"/"Sonar-formative" marks for software/SaaS (incl. the bare mark SONAR) and has actively sued another company (Sonar Software, Inc., Delaware case 1:23-cv-01053) purely for using "Sonar" as a name in the software/SaaS category. Real legal exposure, not just a crowded-name problem — treated as disqualified, not a preference call.

## Side-by-side comparison

| | Kingfisher | Bluefin | Bluewater |
|---|---|---|---|
| Story fit | Strongest — Story 1 + Story 4 | Strong — Story 4 + Story 5 | Strong — Story 5 |
| Emotional register | Skill, patience, craft | Rarity, value, prestige | Expertise, seriousness, calm |
| Explains itself | Instantly (an animal doing something hard) | Instantly (the expensive rare one) | Needs a beat of explanation (sailing term) |
| Non-native friendliness | Good — 3 syllables | Best — 2 syllables | Good, but concept less immediate |
| Legal cleanliness | Unrelated-category conflict only (Kingfisher plc) | Two tech-adjacent conflicts, `.com` taken | Two non-litigious B2B software conflicts, `.com` taken |

**Working recommendation:** Kingfisher, pending a workable trademark opinion — it's the only name that puts "content is still king" inside the word while also dramatizing precision. Bluefin is the safer, more boardroom-neutral pick, and the strongest on pure pronounceability. Bluewater carries the ocean/expertise story most directly but needs the most brand education to land.
