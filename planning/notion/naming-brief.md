# Brand & Naming Brief — LinkedIn Content-Attribution Platform (working project, spun off from Teamfluence)

## 1. Project overview

We are building a new product, spun off from the existing sales-intelligence platform **Teamfluence** (teamfluence.com). Teamfluence today captures LinkedIn "signals" (profile visits, likes, comments, follows, new connections) around a sales team and their outbound activity, and routes them into GTM/CRM workflows — positioned as "Engagement to Sales Pipeline."

This new product reuses Teamfluence's underlying signal-capture and workflow engine, but **repositions it away from outbound sales prospecting and toward content performance and audience discovery for high-end LinkedIn content programs.**

The core mechanic: a company or agency publishes real, high-quality LinkedIn content (from executives, team members, or tracked influencers/keywords). People react to that content — likes, comments, profile visits, new connections. Most of those reactions are noise. Some are real signals from real people who genuinely care. The product finds and surfaces the real ones, filters out the noise, and can (via workflow automation) turn that into outreach, conversations, and meetings.

Internally the project has been code-named **"Top 10%"** — not as a literal brand name, but as a description of the philosophy: we would rather find 10 real, high-quality matches than 1,000 low-quality contacts. Quality over quantity is the central value, not a marketing add-on.

## 2. Target customers

Two segments, both deliberately "high-end" — not targeting solo founders or small, unstructured teams:

1. **Beachhead: boutique, high-end LinkedIn content/ghostwriting agencies.** They manage LinkedIn content for multiple retainer clients (the clients themselves can be smaller companies — we never sell directly to an agency's own clients, to avoid channel conflict). These agencies are thoughtful, not cheap, and need to prove content ROI to keep retainers.
2. **Volume segment: 50+ employee B2B SaaS/tech companies** with real internal marketing/sales/CS structure (e.g. a company like Supermetrics, ~400 people, ~30% in marketing & sales) that run an internal, agency-like content operation around their executives.

Both segments are premium, sales-led, no low-end self-serve tier. The buyer is sophisticated: market leaders (or aspiring to be), who already believe in content and want to know if — and who — it's actually reaching.

## 3. The core idea / positioning

A few beliefs anchor the whole positioning:

- **Content is still king**, even in an age of AI-generated posts and automated outbound. Genuine, well-crafted content still has the power to make a real person stop, read, and respond.
- **We are not a lead generator.** A "lead" is a name on a purchased list. We find real people — people with a real problem, a real interest, who reacted to specific content because it actually meant something to them.
- **Real people have good spam filters.** The people worth reaching have already learned to ignore ads, automation, and cold outreach. If they still engage, that engagement is meaningful — and hard to fake.
- **Quality over quantity, deliberately.** Ten real, qualified matches are worth more than a thousand cold contacts. This is the literal meaning behind the internal code name "Top 10%" — it is a philosophy about precision, not a targeted percentile metric.
- **LinkedIn is an ocean.** Millions of profiles, constant noise, no map. Finding the handful of people who genuinely resonate with a specific piece of content, by hand, is effectively impossible. This product is the instrument that makes it possible.

## 4. The five brand stories

These five short narratives express the same underlying idea from different angles. They are the raw material for finding the product name — the name should feel like it could carry one (or a blend) of these stories.

**Story 1 — "Content is still king. Everything else is noise."**
Everyone can publish. Everyone can automate. AI can write a post in seconds, and bots can comment on a thousand more. But none of that creates a real connection. Real content — written by people who know their craft — still has power. It still makes people stop scrolling. It still makes people answer. We believe content is still king. Our job is to find who's listening to it.

**Story 2 — "Every post sends a signal. We find who catches it."**
When you publish something real, it doesn't just sit there — it reaches people. Someone reads it twice. Someone clicks your profile. Someone comments something that shows they actually understood it. That's a signal. Most tools only count it. We read it, and we find the person behind it.

**Story 3 — "We don't find leads. We find people."**
A lead is a name on a list. A person is someone with a real problem, a real question, and a reason to care about what you wrote. Leads can be bought in bulk. People can't. We build the tools to find the real ones — the ones who read your content because it actually spoke to them.

**Story 4 — "Ten real ones beat a thousand fake ones."**
Most tools are built to find as many contacts as possible. We think that's the wrong game. One person who truly cares about your content is worth more than a thousand who don't. We'd rather hand you ten of the right people than a thousand of the wrong ones.

**Story 5 — "LinkedIn is an ocean. Finding the right people takes real expertise."**
LinkedIn looks small on a screen — a feed, a profile, a like button. It isn't. It's an ocean of hundreds of millions of profiles, constant motion, no map, no way to search it by hand. Most tools just skim the surface, grabbing whatever is easiest to reach. Finding the handful of people who actually matter, in all that noise, takes real instruments and real expertise. That's what we built.

## 5. Task: find the product name

Using the five stories in section 4 (and, if useful, the three adapted proposals in section 5) as creative source material, propose additional candidate product names.

Guidelines / constraints:
- The product is premium/high-end B2B SaaS — the name should feel serious and credible to sophisticated buyers (agency owners, marketing/sales leaders at 50+ employee B2B SaaS companies), not gimmicky or overly playful.
- Roughly 60% of the eventual user base will not be native English speakers. Prefer names that are simple, concrete, and easy to pronounce/understand across languages over abstract or purely academic/technical terms. A little intellectual flavor is fine (the audience works in content/marketing), but avoid names that require explanation.
- The name should evoke at least one of: (a) content/quality still mattering despite automation, (b) finding real, genuine people rather than generated "leads," (c) precision/quality over mass volume, (d) the difficulty of finding the right people in a vast, noisy space (LinkedIn as an "ocean").
- It is acceptable (not required) for a name to subtly hint that the product is built for LinkedIn specifically, without naming LinkedIn directly (e.g. through color association with LinkedIn's blue, or wordplay on "-in").
- Avoid names that read as generic "lead generation" or "sales prospecting" tools (e.g. anything with "leads," "prospect," "pipeline," "outreach" in it) — this directly contradicts the brand's core story (see Story 3).
- Do a basic sanity check for likely name conflicts with existing, reasonably well-known SaaS products before shortlisting (e.g. avoid names that collide with existing tools in adjacent categories).

Deliverable: a shortlist of 10-15 candidate names, each with a one- or two-sentence rationale tying it back to one of the five stories.
